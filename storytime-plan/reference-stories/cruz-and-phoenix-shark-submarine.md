# Cruz, Phoenix and the Shark Submarine

*The next adventure of the magic LEGO brick*

---

It had been a whole week since the magic red brick had taken Cruz and Phoenix back in time to meet the people who invented LEGO.

Every night since then, the boys had checked the brick before bed. Every night, it just sat there, being a brick.

Until tonight.

Phoenix was building an underwater world on the bedroom floor. There was a blue sea made of flat pieces, a little diver, and a big grey shark with a toothy grin.

"Sharks are the coolest," said Cruz.

"Sharks are the BEST," said Phoenix.

And right then, from the bottom of the toy box, came a sound.

**Click.**

The red brick was glowing again.

The boys looked at each other. Then they both dived for the toy box at the same time. The moment their fingers touched the brick, the glow spread across the floor like water... and the LEGO pieces started to **move**.

Bricks flew up into the air. They spun and snapped together, *click-click-click-click*, faster and faster, until standing in the middle of the bedroom was a real, full-sized, bright yellow **submarine** made entirely of LEGO.

It had a round window at the front, a propeller at the back, and two seats inside, just the right size for two boys.

"No way," whispered Cruz.

"WAY!" shouted Phoenix, and climbed straight in.

The hatch closed. The propeller spun. And with a great big **SPLOOSH**, the bedroom floor turned into ocean, and the submarine dived down, down, down into the deep blue sea.

---

## Chapter 1: Grandpa Greenie

The water outside the window was dark and cold, and bits of ice floated near the top.

Something enormous drifted slowly past the window. It was grey-brown, covered in scratches, and moving *so slowly* it looked like it was half asleep.

It turned one big, cloudy eye toward the submarine.

"Well, well," it said in a deep, creaky voice. "Visitors. I haven't had visitors in about... two hundred years."

The boys pressed their noses to the glass.

"Are you a shark?" asked Phoenix.

"I am a **Greenland shark**," said the creature. "But you can call me **Grandpa Greenie**. Everyone does. Mostly because I'm very, very old."

"How old?" asked Cruz.

Grandpa Greenie thought about it for a long time. Greenland sharks do everything slowly, including thinking.

"About **four hundred years**," he said at last.

"FOUR HUNDRED?!" said both boys.

"Greenland sharks can live longer than any other animal with a backbone," said Grandpa Greenie proudly. "Now then. Your brick told me you want to learn about sharks. And the best way to learn about sharks... is to start at the very beginning. Hold on to your seats."

He swished his tail once. The water swirled, the submarine spun round and round, and the whole ocean went **WHOOOOSH**...

---

## Chapter 2: Older Than Trees

When the spinning stopped, the water was warm and green, and the boys could see the sandy bottom.

"Where are we?" asked Cruz.

"Not *where*," said Grandpa Greenie, swimming up beside them. "*When*. We've gone back in time. A **very, very, very long time**. Hundreds of millions of years."

Phoenix looked out at the land above the water. It was bare, rocky and empty.

"There aren't any trees," he said.

"That's right," said Grandpa Greenie. "Trees haven't been invented yet. And dinosaurs? They won't show up for ages."

"So what's here?" asked Cruz.

A little shape zipped past the window. Then another. They had pointy noses and swishy tails.

"**Sharks**," said Grandpa Greenie. "Sharks were swimming in the oceans **before there were trees**, and **long before the dinosaurs**. My great-great-great-great... well, lots and lots of greats... grandparents."

"Sharks are older than DINOSAURS?" said Phoenix.

"Much older," said Grandpa Greenie. "And here's another secret: sharks don't have any bones."

Cruz frowned. "Then how do they stay shark-shaped?"

"Squeeze the end of your nose," said Grandpa Greenie.

Cruz squeezed. It bent and wiggled.

"That bendy stuff is called **cartilage**. It's in your nose and your ears. And a shark's whole skeleton is made of it! It makes sharks light and bendy, so they can twist and turn in the water."

Both boys spent the next minute wiggling their noses and ears and giggling.

---

## Chapter 3: The Shark With a Table on Its Head

**WHOOOOSH!** The submarine spun forward in time.

A funny-looking shark swam up to the window. It had something sticking up from its back that was flat and bristly on top, like a scrubbing brush... or a tiny ironing board.

"What is THAT?" laughed Phoenix.

"That," said Grandpa Greenie, "is **Stethacanthus**. It lived a very long time ago. Scientists still aren't quite sure what the flat part was for. Maybe for showing off!"

"It looks like it's carrying a table," said Cruz.

"And look over there," said Grandpa Greenie.

Another shark swam by. When it opened its mouth, the boys saw that its bottom teeth were curled up in a big **spiral**, like a snail shell made of teeth.

"That's **Helicoprion**," said Grandpa Greenie. "The buzz-saw shark."

"Whoa," said the boys together.

"Sharks have tried out lots of shapes over the years," said Grandpa Greenie. "Some worked. Some were a bit silly. But sharks kept going, and kept changing, for millions and millions of years."

---

## Chapter 4: The Biggest Shark Ever

**WHOOOOSH!**

This time, the water went very, very quiet. All the little fish had swum away to hide.

"Um," said Phoenix. "Why is everybody hiding?"

A shadow slid over the submarine. It kept going. And going. And going. It was so long that it took ages to pass by.

"Boys," whispered Grandpa Greenie. "Meet **Megalodon**."

It was the biggest shark the boys had ever seen, far bigger than a bus. It swam slowly past, not bothering the little yellow submarine at all.

Something small and shiny floated down onto the sand beside them. Cruz used the submarine's LEGO grabber arm to pick it up.

It was a **tooth**. And it was bigger than Cruz's whole hand.

"Megalodon was the biggest shark that ever lived," said Grandpa Greenie. "Those teeth are so strong that people still dig them up today."

"Where is Megalodon now?" asked Phoenix.

"All gone," said Grandpa Greenie. "Megalodon disappeared millions of years ago. The oceans got colder, and its favourite foods became hard to find. But other sharks survived. And one of the biggest surprises is this: sharks even survived the giant disaster that ended the **dinosaurs**!"

"Sharks are TOUGH," said Cruz.

"Sharks are SURVIVORS," said Grandpa Greenie.

---

## Chapter 5: The Shark Parade

**WHOOOOSH!**

The submarine popped out of the swirl into a bright blue ocean full of sunlight and coral, all the colours of a LEGO box.

"Welcome," said Grandpa Greenie, "to **today**. There are more than **five hundred** different kinds of sharks in the oceans right now. Let me introduce a few friends."

First came a shark so HUGE that the boys gasped. It was covered in white spots, like someone had flicked paint all over it. It swam with its mouth wide open.

"Is it going to eat us?" squeaked Phoenix.

"The **whale shark**? Oh no," chuckled Grandpa Greenie. "It's the **biggest fish in the whole sea**, but it only eats teeny-tiny food called **plankton**. It just opens its mouth and scoops it up like a giant vacuum cleaner. And every whale shark has its own pattern of spots, like a fingerprint."

Next, a shark swam by with a head shaped like a big flat letter **T**.

"A **hammerhead**!" shouted Cruz.

"With its eyes way out on the sides of its head, it can see almost all the way around," said Grandpa Greenie. "And it has a special superpower. All sharks can feel the tiny bits of electricity that come from animals' bodies. With its wide head, the hammerhead is extra good at it. It can find fish hiding under the sand!"

Then came a shark with stripes, like a tiger.

"The **tiger shark**," said Grandpa Greenie. "It will try eating nearly anything. People have found tyres, tin cans and even car number plates in tiger sharks' tummies!"

"Yuck!" said Phoenix, delighted.

Next, something tiny wiggled right up to the window. It was so small it could fit in Phoenix's hand. And its tummy was **glowing**.

"That's a **dwarf lanternshark**," said Grandpa Greenie. "One of the smallest sharks in the world. It lives deep down where it's dark, so it makes its own light!"

"It's like a nightlight shark," said Phoenix.

And last of all, in a shallow rock pool, a spotty little shark did something amazing. It pushed itself up on its fins and... **walked**.

"Sharks can WALK?!" said Cruz.

"The **epaulette shark** can," said Grandpa Greenie. "It uses its fins like little legs to walk between rock pools when the tide goes out."

---

## Chapter 6: The Great White

Finally, out of the deep blue, came a shark the boys knew straight away. It was grey on top, white underneath, and had a big pointy fin.

"A **great white**," whispered Cruz.

The shark swam right up to the window and looked in. Then it opened its mouth. Rows and rows of teeth!

Phoenix held Cruz's hand. But the great white didn't bite. It just swam slowly around the submarine, curious.

"Great whites are amazing hunters," said Grandpa Greenie. "They can smell a tiny drop of blood from very far away. And do you want to know a secret about all those teeth?"

The boys nodded.

"Sharks lose teeth all the time. But whenever a tooth falls out, a new one moves forward to take its place, like a conveyor belt. A shark can have **thousands** of teeth in its lifetime!"

"The Tooth Fairy must be SO busy," said Phoenix.

Grandpa Greenie laughed so hard that bubbles came out of his gills.

"And one more thing," he said, getting serious. "People are often scared of sharks. But sharks almost never want to eat people. People aren't their food. The truth is, sharks are the ones who need looking after. Lots of sharks are in danger because of people fishing too many of them. Sharks help keep the whole ocean healthy. So the ocean needs sharks, and sharks need us to be kind to them."

The great white gave one swish of its tail, almost like a wave goodbye, and disappeared into the blue.

---

## Chapter 7: Home Again

The red brick on the submarine's control panel began to glow.

"Time for you to go home," said Grandpa Greenie. "Before your dad finds two empty beds."

"Will we see you again?" asked Phoenix.

"I'm four hundred years old," said Grandpa Greenie with a slow wink. "I'll still be here next time."

**WHOOOOSH!**

And there they were, back on the bedroom floor. The submarine had turned back into a pile of LEGO pieces. The red brick sat quietly on top, like it had never done anything special at all.

But next to it, on the carpet, was something that hadn't been there before.

It was a **tooth**. A big, grey, triangle-shaped tooth.

Cruz picked it up and turned it over in his hands.

"Older than dinosaurs," he said quietly.

"Older than TREES," said Phoenix.

Then the boys looked at each other and grinned. They picked up their LEGO shark, gave it a little swim across the floor, and whispered together:

"Goodnight, Grandpa Greenie."

---

## The End

*Goodnight, Cruz. Goodnight, Phoenix. Swim well.*

### True facts from the story

- Sharks have been around for **hundreds of millions of years**. They're **older than trees** and **older than the dinosaurs**.
- Sharks have **no bones**. Their skeletons are made of **cartilage**, like your nose and ears.
- **Stethacanthus** was an ancient shark with a flat, brush-topped fin on its back. **Helicoprion** had a spiral of teeth.
- **Megalodon** was the biggest shark ever. Its teeth could be bigger than a grown-up's hand, and it died out millions of years ago.
- Sharks **survived** the event that ended the dinosaurs.
- There are **more than 500 kinds of sharks** today.
- The **whale shark** is the biggest fish in the sea and eats tiny plankton. Its spots are unique, like fingerprints.
- **Hammerheads** see almost all the way around, and all sharks can sense tiny electric signals from other animals.
- **Tiger sharks** eat almost anything, including rubbish like tyres and number plates.
- The **dwarf lanternshark** is one of the smallest sharks and **glows in the dark**.
- The **epaulette shark** can **walk** on its fins.
- **Greenland sharks** can live for **hundreds of years**, maybe around 400.
- Sharks **replace their teeth** all their lives and can go through thousands.
- Sharks rarely bother people, and many shark species need protecting.
